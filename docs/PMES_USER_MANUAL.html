Introduction

Why this system exists and what it replaces.

Assessing personnel fairly requires more than a form. It requires that the same instrument be applied to everyone, that each person be rated by the right set of colleagues, that scores be computed identically every time, and that the whole record be traceable afterwards. Doing that on paper or in separate spreadsheets produces exactly the problems it is meant to prevent: inconsistent forms, missing raters, arithmetic errors, and results that cannot be reconstructed.

The Innovations Performance Assessment Tool (IPAT), issued under Administrative Order No. 11, series of 2025, prescribes a single multi-rater instrument for personnel in the Innovation Cluster. It measures three domains - behaviour, output, and fitness for the job - and combines them into one numerical and adjectival rating.

The Performance Monitoring and Evaluation System (PMES) is the Department's implementation of that instrument. It was developed and validated in the Social Technology Bureau, and following its presentation to the Undersecretary it is being adopted across all offices of the Innovation Cluster.

Unless a section says otherwise, it applies to everyone. Only the three sections written for administrators carry a marker - Office Administrator or Central Administration. If you do not hold that access, the corresponding menu items will not appear for you and those sections may be skipped.

Before issuance

Items shown in dotted gold must be completed by the implementing office before this manual is circulated - the portal address, support contacts, and the assessment period covered by the current rollout.

About the System

One system, two names, depending on which office you belong to.

PMES is a web application. There is nothing to install: you open it in a browser, sign in with your DSWD account, and work from there. Your data is held centrally and each participating office sees only its own personnel and results.

The system names itself according to who is signed in. Personnel of the Social Technology Bureau see Performance Monitoring and Evaluation System, which includes the Bureau's own IPCRF and CCEF instruments. Personnel of other participating offices see the Innovation Cluster Personnel Assessment Portal - the same system, showing only the assessment functions their office has adopted. Both are described in this manual; sections that apply to only one are marked.

What PMES does

  • Holds the approved assessment indicators as published content, so every rater in every office answers the same questions.
  • Determines who rates whom, from rules your office configures rather than from a hierarchy fixed in the software.
  • Collects ratings from each assigned rater privately, with drafts that can be saved and resumed.
  • Computes the weighted score across the three domains and assigns the adjectival descriptor.
  • Shows each person their own consolidated result, without revealing who rated them or how each rater scored.
  • Gives administrators completion monitoring and exportable reports for their office, and central administration a view across the whole cluster.
  • Records administrative actions in an audit trail.

What PMES does not do

  • It does not decide who is assessed. Your office administrator maintains the roster.
  • It does not change the approved instrument. Indicators and weights come from the issuance, not from the software.
  • It is not a document-routing or filing system.
3

Objectives

What the Department intends the system to achieve.

Uniform application

Apply one approved instrument, with one set of indicators and one computation, to every person assessed across the Cluster.

Complete multi-rater coverage

Ensure each person is rated by the full set of relationships the instrument requires - self, peers, subordinates, and supervisors.

Confidentiality

Keep individual rater identities and individual rater scores private, releasing only the consolidated result to the person assessed.

Progress visibility

Let administrators see completion in real time and follow up on outstanding ratings before the period closes.

Reliable computation

Remove manual arithmetic from scoring, so results are reproducible and auditable.

Evidence for decisions

Produce exportable analytics that support development planning and Cluster-level reporting.

Until an administrator activates your account you will see a pending screen at every sign-in. Nothing further is required from you. If it persists beyond a working day, follow up with your office administrator.

If an administrator created your account for you, you receive a temporary password. At your first sign-in PMES presents a password-change prompt that cannot be dismissed until you set your own password. Choose a password you do not use on any other system and do not write it down where others can see it.

5.6 If you forget your password

Password reset is administrator-assisted

PMES does not currently send self-service password reset emails. Contact your office administrator, who will issue a new temporary password. You will be required to change it at your next sign-in.

If you normally use Continue with Google, there is no PMES password to reset - recover your Google account through ICTMS instead.

5.7 Automatic overnight sign-out

Every session is signed out automatically at midnight, Philippine time, whether or not the browser was left open. This is deliberate: it prevents an unattended workstation from holding an open session overnight. If you find yourself returned to the sign-in page in the morning, this is why - sign in again and continue. Drafts you have saved are not affected.

5.8 Signing out

  1. Open your profile menuSelect your name or profile icon at the top of the screen.
  2. Select Sign outYou are returned to the sign-in page.

Sign out at the end of every session, particularly on a shared workstation.

Understanding Your Access Level

PMES shows different menus to different people. This explains which you are.

Menu items are not merely hidden by access level - the system refuses the underlying request as well. If a colleague describes a screen you cannot see, it is because your access level does not include it, not because your browser is faulty.

Access levelWho holds itWhat it can do
Participating personnel Everyone being assessed in a participating office Complete assigned ratings, view own consolidated results, read the Assessment Library and Rating Guide, view own personal information
Office administrator The focal person designated by each participating office Everything above, plus: maintain the office structure and personnel roster, tag raters, monitor office completion, generate office reports
Supervisory monitor Division Chiefs and Section Heads Everything personnel can do, plus view the office assessment dashboard and roster for their scope
Central administration Cluster-level administrators Register and provision participating offices, monitor completion across the Cluster, generate Cluster-wide reports
System administrator Designated STB system administrator All of the above, plus user management, assessment content, and the audit trail
Being a supervisor is not the same as being an administrator

Supervisors rate their staff and can see completion progress, but they do not assign raters or edit the roster. Those are office administrator functions, granted separately.

Completing an Assessment

The rating form itself - the central task for every user.

10.1 Opening the form

From My Rating Tasks, select Start Rating or Continue on the row for the person you are rating. Confirm the name at the top of the form before you begin.

The form is organised into domains, listed down the left side. Each shows how many indicators you have answered out of the total, so you can see at a glance what remains. Depending on your rater relationship, you may not be asked for every domain.

10.2 The rating scale

Behavioural and job-fitness indicators use a four-point frequency scale. You are judging how often the behaviour is demonstrated, not forming an overall opinion of the person.

ScoreDescriptorApply when
4AlwaysThe behaviour is consistently demonstrated, with clear and repeated evidence.
3FrequentlyThe behaviour is demonstrated more often than not.
2RarelyThe behaviour is demonstrated only occasionally.
1NeverThe behaviour is not demonstrated.
Rate on evidence, not impression

Rate what you have actually observed over the assessment period. Where you have not observed a behaviour at all, say so through the scale rather than awarding a middle score to avoid the question - a defaulted 3 distorts the result as much as an unfair 1.

10.3 Core Behavioral Competencies

This domain covers the HEARTWORK values. Each value carries several behavioural indicators, and you rate every indicator on the four-point scale. The indicator text is the approved wording - read it fully rather than rating from the value's title alone.

This is the longest domain. The progress bar beside it in the left-hand list shows how far through you are.

10.4 Functional Performance and Output

This domain is not rated indicator by indicator. It carries the person's final numerical rating from their individual performance commitment and review form. Where the Bureau's IPCRF module is in use the figure is synchronised automatically; otherwise the office administrator encodes it, on a scale of 1 to 5.

As an ordinary rater you will normally see this domain as read-only. If it is blank, the office administrator has not yet supplied the rating - this does not block your own submission.

10.5 Job Fitness

A short set of indicators on the same four-point scale, covering alignment between the person's qualifications, competencies, and work habits and the requirements of the position - including educational qualifications, relevant work experience, training records, and attendance and punctuality.

Only the ratee and the immediate supervisor rate this domain, in coordination with the designated HR focal, who validates the supporting records. Peers, subordinates, and the skip-level supervisor are not asked for it, and the tab will not appear in their forms. See 12.2.

10.6 Saving a draft

Save as you go, especially on the behavioural domain. Saving keeps your entries without submitting them, and the task appears as Draft so you can return to it. If your connection drops, entries saved to that point are kept; anything typed since your last save may be lost.

10.7 Submitting

  1. Check every domain is completeThe left-hand list shows answered against total for each domain. Required indicators must be answered before the form will submit.
  2. Review your entriesMove back through the domains and confirm each rating is what you intend.
  3. Select Submit RatingsOn a phone this button is pinned to the bottom of the screen so you do not have to scroll to the end to reach it.
  4. Confirm the status changeThe task moves to Submitted in My Rating Tasks.
Submitting is final

A submitted rating is locked and cannot be edited. If a correction is genuinely needed, ask your office administrator to reopen the task. Reopening is recorded in the audit trail.

My Results

Your own consolidated result, and what is deliberately not shown.

My Results shows your overall score and adjectival descriptor for each assessment period, together with your score in each of the three domains. Where you have results from more than one period, tabs across the top let you select which period to view - this doubles as your assessment history.

Rater confidentiality

Individual rater identities and individual rater scores are never shown to the person assessed. You see only the consolidated figures. This applies to everyone, including administrators viewing their own results.

11.1 Results that are not yet final

If some of your assigned raters have not yet submitted, an amber notice states how many of the total have. The consolidated score is computed once all ratings are in, so any figure shown before that point is provisional.

11.2 If a deduction has been applied

Where the approved protocol provides for a deduction, the result carries a note to that effect. The system does not print the reasoning; ask your office administrator for an explanation of the adjustment.

11.3 If nothing appears

An empty screen means no result has been finalised for you yet. Results appear once every assigned rater has submitted and the period's result has been finalised.

How the Score is Computed

The arithmetic behind the number, stated plainly.

You do not need this section to complete a rating. It is here so that anyone who asks how a result was arrived at can be given a straight answer.

12.1 Domain weights

Overall assessment score
Overall = (CBC × 0.30) + (FPO × 0.55) + (JF × 0.15)

CBC - Core Behavioral Competencies. FPO - Functional Performance and Output. JF - Job Fitness. Where a domain has no data for a person, it is excluded and the remaining weights are rescaled, so a result computed from two domains is still valid.

An office may adopt adjusted weights only where the approved protocol allows it, all three domains remain represented, and the basis for the adjustment is recorded in the system alongside the approving authority.

12.2 Who rates which domain

The three domains do not use the same raters. This is the single most misread rule in the instrument, so it is set out plainly:

DomainWho rates itHow it is combined
Core Behavioral Competencies All five relationships - self, peer, subordinate, immediate supervisor, skip-level supervisor Weighted, per the table in 12.3
Functional Performance and Output Not rated by raters at all - carried from the approved performance tool (IPCR / DPCR) Taken as the final numerical rating
Job Fitness The ratee and the immediate supervisor only, in coordination with the HR focal, who validates the supporting records A simple average of the two
Job Fitness has only two raters

Peers, subordinates, and the skip-level supervisor do not rate Job Fitness. The domain is evidence- and document-based - educational qualifications, work experience, training records, attendance and punctuality - so it is assessed only by the person and their immediate supervisor, with the HR focal validating the records.

If you are assigned as a peer, subordinate, or skip-level supervisor, the Job Fitness tab will not appear in your form. That is correct behaviour, not a fault.

Job Fitness
Indicator score = (Self-Rating + Immediate Supervisor Rating) ÷ 2

The domain score is then the sum of the indicator scores divided by the number of indicators. Where the ratee's self-rating and the immediate supervisor's differ by more than the office's threshold - 1.00 point on the 1-4 scale unless the office has adopted and documented a different figure - the record is referred to the skip-level supervisor, who receives a notification. They may require additional documentation, discuss the matter with those concerned, or recommend a developmental, deployment, or performance management action.

12.3 How the behavioural raters are combined

This weighting applies to Core Behavioral Competencies only. Each indicator is scored by combining the raters at these weights, and the domain score is the average of the indicator scores.

Rater relationshipWeightNote
Immediate Supervisor30%Largest single share.
Skip-Level Supervisor25%The supervisor of the ratee's immediate supervisor.
Self-assessment15%
Peer15%A peer within the division or section.
Subordinate15%Applies only where the ratee has subordinates.
Total100%Five raters.
Where the ratee has no subordinates

A second peer rater from the same division or section is designated instead, and the subordinate's 15% is reassigned to them. Each of the two peers then carries 15%, for a combined peer weight of 30%. The subordinate share is never simply dropped, and one peer never carries 30% alone.

12.4 Adjectival descriptors

Final Numerical RatingQualitative DescriptorGeneral Interpretation
4.00OutstandingPerfect score across all KPIs and competencies.
3.50 - 3.99Very SatisfactoryExceeds expected standards and primary goals.
2.75 - 3.49SatisfactoryConsistently meets job expectations and targets.
2.00 - 2.74Needs ImprovementInconsistent performance; meets some targets, requires coaching.
1.00 - 1.99Requires Immediate InterventionFails key criteria; performance improvement plan required.
Why the output rating uses a different scale

Behavioural and job-fitness indicators are rated 1 to 4. The performance output rating arrives from the individual performance review on a 1 to 5 scale and is converted to the same 1 to 4 basis before it is combined, so the three domains are comparable.

Office Administration

Setting up an office, validating its roster, tagging raters, and monitoring progress.

Office AdministratorSupervisors (view only)
Order matters

Complete these in sequence: structure, then personnel, then rater tagging. Assignments generated against an incomplete roster will have gaps, and correcting them afterwards means reopening submitted work.

13.1 Office Structure

Found under Office Management → Structure. Here you define the divisions, sections, and roles your office offers. These become the options personnel choose from when they register, so set them up before inviting anyone to register.

  1. List your divisions or unitsUse the names your office actually uses in its issuances, not abbreviations invented for the system.
  2. Add sections under each divisionSections appear only after their parent division is selected during registration.
  3. Set the roles offered at registrationThese are the roles personnel may request. You still confirm each one on approval.

13.2 Personnel Validation

Found under Office Management → Personnel. This is the assessment roster: the definitive list of who is assessed in your office. Counters across the top show total personnel, active, for validation, and inactive.

  1. Review those awaiting validationUse the For Validation tab. Each entry is someone who registered and is waiting on you.
  2. Check the detailsName as it should appear on records, position, division or unit, section, and role. These drive rater assignment, so correct them now rather than later.
  3. Activate or correctActivate the record, or edit it first if the person selected the wrong division or requested a role they do not hold.
  4. Add anyone missingUse Add Personnel for staff who have not registered themselves.
  5. Deactivate those who have leftSet the record inactive so no assignments are generated for them.
The roster is the foundation

Every rating assignment is derived from this roster. A person listed under the wrong division will be given the wrong peers; a person missing from it will not be assessed at all.

13.3 Rater Tagging

Found at Rater Tagging. This is where you declare, for each role in your office, which roles supply that person's raters. The automated generator reads these rules - it has no built-in hierarchy of its own. A role left unconfigured produces no assignments for the personnel holding it.

For each role you configure one row per rater type:

ColumnWhat to enter
Rater TypeThe relationship being defined - self, peer, subordinate, immediate supervisor, or skip-level supervisor.
Drawn From Role(s)Which role or roles supply that rater.
Where To LookThe scope searched - the same section, the same division, or the whole office.
If Nobody FoundWhat the generator should do when no eligible person exists - widen the search or leave the assignment unmade.

The counters at the top of the screen show roles configured, roles not configured, and how many personnel are affected by the gaps. Aim for zero roles not configured before generating assignments.

Technical staff and the subordinate rater

The supplied defaults give a technical staff member two peer raters and no subordinate. Under the protocol this is the correct arrangement for an office that has no administrative support staff distinct from its technical staff: where subordinate rating is not applicable, an additional peer is designated and the subordinate's 15% passes to them.

Where your office does have administrative support staff, the protocol instead names them as the subordinate rater for technical staff. To adopt that arrangement:

  1. Add the roleIn Office Management → Structure, add Administrative Staff to the roles offered at registration. It is not offered by default.
  2. Re-tag the personnelIn Office Management → Personnel, set your administrative support staff to that role. Until this is done they are indistinguishable from technical staff and will be drawn as peers.
  3. Replace the peer rowsIn Rater Tagging, for the Technical Staff role, remove the Peer1 and Peer2 rows and add a single Peer row (drawn from Technical Staff) plus a Subordinate row drawn from Administrative Staff. Set both to search the same section, falling back to the division.
Replace the peer rows - do not simply add a subordinate

Leaving Peer1 and Peer2 in place while adding a Subordinate row produces six raters where the protocol has five, and the weights would no longer total 100%. Configure exactly one peer alongside the subordinate.

Changing rater rules mid-period

Regenerating assignments after personnel have started rating invalidates work already submitted against the old arrangement. Make these changes between assessment periods wherever possible.

Job Fitness variance referrals

Where a ratee's Job Fitness self-rating and their immediate supervisor's differ by more than the office threshold, the system notifies the skip-level supervisor assigned to that person and shows the gap on the record. The referral is sent once per record, not repeatedly, and is written to the audit trail.

The threshold defaults to 1.00 point on the 1-4 scale. The protocol requires review where responses differ significantly but sets no figure, so an office may adopt a different threshold provided it records the basis - the same condition the protocol attaches to adjusted domain weights.

If no skip supervisor is assigned

A referral cannot be sent for a ratee who has no skip-level supervisor in the current period's assignments. Check the Rater Tagging coverage counters before the period opens - a role with no skip supervisor configured will silently produce no referrals.

13.4 Office Assessment Dashboard

Found at Office Dashboard. Your monitoring view for the current period. It reports:

  • Completion for the office, and by division or section using the scope tabs.
  • Top Outstanding and Top Needs Improvement, for development planning.
  • Pending Rating Workload - who is holding up completion, so follow-up can be targeted rather than broadcast.

Selecting any panel opens the detail behind it.

Central Administration

The register of participating offices. Each row shows the office, its primary administrator, its office status, its portal status, and when it was last validated. Onboarding an office follows a fixed sequence:

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I change a rating after I submit it?

No. A submitted rating is locked. If a correction is genuinely needed, ask your office administrator to reopen the task; reopening is recorded in the audit trail.

Will the person I rate see my scores?

No. Individual rater identities and individual rater scores are not shown to the person assessed. They see only the consolidated result.

What happens if I lose connection while rating?

Entries already saved as a draft are kept. Anything typed since your last save may be lost, so save periodically on the longer domains.

When will I see my own results?

Once every assigned rater has submitted and the result has been finalised for the period.

Someone I should be rating is missing from my list.

Assignments are generated by your office administrator. Contact them so the assignment can be added for the current period.

I forgot my password.

Contact your office administrator, who will issue a temporary password. You will be required to change it at your next sign-in. If you sign in with Google, recover your Google account through ICTMS instead.

I did not receive a verification code.

PMES does not send one. Sign-in requires only your DSWD account. Treat any message claiming to be a PMES code as suspicious.

Why was I signed out overnight?

Every session is signed out at midnight, Philippine time, by design. Sign in again; your saved drafts are unaffected.

A colleague can see a menu item I cannot.

Menus follow access level. See section 6. If you believe your access level is wrong, raise it with your office administrator.

Can I use PMES on my phone?

Yes. The screens adapt to narrow displays, and on the rating form the submit button stays pinned within reach.

My position or division is wrong on my dashboard.

These come from the roster your office administrator maintains. Ask them to correct the record - the same details drive your rating assignments.

Do I have to finish a rating in one sitting?

No. Save a draft and return to it. The task stays in your list as Draft until you submit.

18

Security and User Responsibility

Account security

Keep your password confidential. Never share credentials or let a colleague rate on your behalf - every submission is attributed to the account that made it.

Session hygiene

Sign out at the end of every session, particularly on shared workstations. Do not leave a rating form open and unattended.

Data privacy

Assessment records are personal information under the Data Privacy Act. Access only what your duties require and never disclose another person's result.

Rater confidentiality

Do not disclose how you rated a colleague, and do not press anyone to disclose how they rated you. Confidentiality is what makes multi-rater assessment honest.

Accurate records

Administrators are responsible for the accuracy of the roster. An incorrect record produces incorrect assignments and, ultimately, an incorrect result.

Official use only

Use PMES solely for official business. Administrative actions are recorded in the audit trail and are attributable.

Report anything suspicious

If you receive a message asking for your PMES password, a verification code, or directing you to a sign-in page at an address other than the official one, do not respond. Report it to your ICT focal immediately.
